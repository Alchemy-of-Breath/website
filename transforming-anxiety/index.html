Does this sound familiar?

Do you worry excessively?

Does it keep you up at night, prevent you from engaging in social situations and trying new things, or interfere with your work or relationships? If you feel on edge, irritable, or overwhelmed, even just reading this, then please read on.

In fact, if you can answer 'yes' to any of the above, you are not alone — and we commend you for making the courageous choice to seek out a solution for yourself.

  • Sleepless nights, with a mind that will not switch off
  • Avoiding social situations, or anything unfamiliar
  • Anxiety interfering with your work and your relationships
  • Feeling permanently on edge, irritable, or overwhelmed

All of the above are symptoms of anxiety — the most common mental illness in the world.

What if there's another way?

Anxiety is not something wrong with you.

Calling it a mental illness implies there is something wrong with us, when in fact it is often an inner reflection of the outer chaos around us. Between the news, global instability, poverty, global warming, corrupt politics, societal and domestic violence, and what can feel like a daily struggle to survive — it's no wonder so many of us are anxious.

The very fact that you feel anxiety says that you therefore have the power to change it.

In this anxiety management training course I will show you how to work with the physical, emotional, and spiritual body, the brain, and the nervous system; as well as the traumas and negative subconscious programming which operate as the fuel for anxiety.

Have you ever decided to buy a car in a particular colour and then begun to see that same car everywhere? This happens because of a part of the brain called the Reticular Activating System (RAS). Are you ready to consciously use that RAS to create the results you want?

And this is not fringe thinking: there have been thousands of studies over the past decade showing breath exercises are either equal to or superior to medications and other standard medical practices for the treatment of anxiety disorders, stress, PTSD, and depression.
