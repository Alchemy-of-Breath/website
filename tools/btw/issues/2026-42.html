Dear friend,

Most of us know what it feels like to chase after something or someone. Or be chased for validation, approval, money, and the list goes on.

Slippery. Elusive. It’s a whole lot of hard work.

And often, underneath it all there’s an ache, a deep hurt, that we are somehow not enough, and we need to convince someone we’re good, worthy, or — enough.

Sigh…

Then there are moments when things simply flow. You meet the right person at the right time. Something you’ve been longing for finds its way to you with ease, grace, perhaps even a little serendipity — the opposite of chasing, or being chased.

These experiences teach me to feel the difference between convincing and conviction — not just in my mind, but in my body.

Convincing is the chase. The pushing, proving, persuading. The exhausting dance of trying to get something or someone to meet a need we may not even know we have.

Conviction feels and looks different.

It’s not loud, flashy, or made of fanfare. It’s grounded. Still and silent. It’s a deep inner knowing that doesn’t need validation to feel worthy.

So why do we work so hard to get our needs met through convincing?

Perhaps because we don’t always know what we really — need. Without clocking what we really need, we may not know our true value… so then we chase it in people, places, and things.

It’s the opposite of innate conviction. Without it, we can find ourselves trying to get what we need through pushing, persuading, or unconsciously manipulating situations and people.

“Like me”

“See me”

“Validate me”

Not because we’re manipulative people. But because we’re human, and sometimes we simply don’t know what our needs are, or our value…. much less how to communicate our needs in a way that gets them met.

The irony is that convincing cancels out conviction. Like inner apology robs inner authority.

Try this for a moment.

Bring to mind a situation where you’re trying to convince someone of something.

Notice what happens in your body.

Now imagine letting go of the need to convince. Not giving up on what matters to you, but releasing the struggle to make it happen.

What changes?

One state might feel contracted, depleted, and uncertain. The other, grounded, solid, and secure.

What is the longing beneath the chase? What need is asking to be seen?
