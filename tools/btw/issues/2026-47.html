Dear friend,

Do you ever hear yourself saying: “he’ll never change,” or “if only she would change.”

This is one of my definitions of pain: thinking others need to change, and the reoccurring disappointment — when they don’t meet what I think they should do.

After my first marriage ended, 24 years ago, I cried every day for three years. Then one day it hit me… one of those big “aha” moments.

Without realizing it, I had been waiting for him to change. I blamed him for our problems, and some part of me insisted he was the one who had to change.

I can still remember that pain clearly… not only the projection onto him, though more — the pain of negating my own personal power — as if it was all up to him (if only he did what I wanted him to do).

Duh, I said to myself when I finally got that the only person I can change is ME.

I hadn’t yet understood that my expectations were really the source of my pain, not him. And most important, the position I put myself in continously to be hurt and disappointed, like a repeat of my childhood in essence.

The day it finally landed, I remember putting my hand to my head and crying out loud:

“NOBODY NEEDS TO CHANGE FOR ME.”

That was the day I set myself free.

I stopped blaming, took responsibility for my own life and happiness, let go a lot… and opened up to the very different life I have today.

What would happen in your life if you let go of needing anyone to be different for you?

I know it can sound bitter (sweet?)… though its ultimately liberating, even if there is much to face. It gets easier when we bring our breath to it — intentionally.

May we be the change we want to see in others.

Let’s give ourselves the freedom to let go, free ourselves and others, and to see what becomes possible when we stop expecting others to change.

My love and support to you, always,

Amy
