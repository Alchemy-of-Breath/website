Or perhaps the better question is…

Dear friend,

So often, we outsource trust.

We hand over the keys to our wellbeing, our feelings, our sense of what’s okay and what’s not — to someone — or something, else. Sometimes with a ribbon on top.

And then, when things go pear-shaped, we find ourselves asking:

Who can I trust?

A very good question.

But perhaps not the first question.

If this is you right now, what would it mean to gather up all those keys you’ve handed out over the years, pop them back into your own pocket, and begin to deeply trust yourself?

Not the “when I’m good enough” version of yourself. The real you. Messy sometimes, uncertain at other times — human. The one who sometimes knows better, but does it anyway. The one who ignored a red flag because it looked how we wanted it to — rather than what it really is.

Yes. That one.

Because when we truly trust ourselves, something rather beautiful happens. A flowering of what otherwise lies dormant. An opening we yearn for, though we may have never dared to let go enough to experience it.

Trust is no longer something we scatter like confetti, hoping it lands in the right hands, in the best way — without knowing if it’s even warranted or not.

It becomes something sacred. Something earned. Something felt.

And when others merit our trust, it’s likely because our own self-trust has set the standard, and we wouldn’t have it any other way.

What if the deepest trust doesn’t begin with another, but with the still knowing of our own worth?

A knowing that doesn’t need applause, approval, or someone else’s validation.

A knowing that lives within.

And yet, how often do we offer others the trust we haven’t quite granted ourselves?

No wonder life keeps sending us opportunities to practice discernment. Sometimes dressed as romance, friendship, or a business opportunity — that all may benefit from a witnessing eye — that’s watching from the lens of trust within… first, not later.

Life does have a rather wicked sense of humour.

And each time, the invitation is the same:

to see things as they are, not as we wish them to be

to listen to the whisper of that little voice within before it becomes a shout

to recognise that our value isn’t something another person, place, or thing gets to determine, or direct

And perhaps, to stop asking the universe for another sign when we’ve already received plenty.

So maybe the question isn’t:

“Who can I trust?”

But rather:

“Do I trust myself?”

Because when trust is earned, there’s often little question. Have we earned our own trust? Probably. Though we may not have taken stock of it.

And what about when we keep giving away our precious trust without it being merited?

Well… the lessons in self-value tend to arrive in abundance. Sometimes with sequels. And that’s a good thing.

Even if we might prefer our lessons to be easy, rather than another full-blown spiritual initiation.

So, dear one…

What’s possible for you with full self-trust? How might your relationships change? Your choices? Your decisions? The direction of your life?

Imagine a life where your heart stays open, and your discernment has a seat at the table, and protects you — without needing to be a bulldog.

Where love flows freely, but your worth is no longer up for grabs. Where trust begins at home. Within you.

Because sometimes the person we’ve been waiting to trust has been patiently waiting for us all along.

Bless your courage. Your dedication. Your willingness to keep opening your heart, even when life has given it a good old shake.

And bless this ever-deepening journey of self-love… and self-trust.

With love,

Amy
