Tricky one, huh?

So often we have a strong feeling, reaction, or perhaps a knee-jerk defense that arises when something someone says or does lands in a weird way inside of us.

And if it hits deep enough and we lose our critical thinking — or perhaps even self-responsibility — we can even swear that someone is either out to get us, doesn’t care, or is just a plain a-hole.

Yet, are we responding from a calm, considered place, or are we reacting from a triggered, irritated place that struck a nerve that may be worthy of investigation?

At this point, we may not question if how we took it was actually factual, or a feeling.

In that reactive place, we can find every reason in the world to justify how we feel. And not to say that isn’t valid at times, though without slowing down, taking a few breaths, and deeply feeling into what just happened, we can defend feelings as facts, and lose track of the real opportunity because we feel wounded on some level.

Stay with me….

I know this may not be an easy one.

Sometimes we don’t need facts to justify what we feel. What we feel can certainly stand alone, and needs no validation other than our own.

Yet, feelings can also mislead us if we’re not willing to take in the bare bone — unbiased — facts as well.

It doesn’t mean it will take the sting out of the situation, though if I’m willing to take an extra breath, and not be too quick to react back — then I am more likely to see a fuller picture than the one my feelings first trigger.

For example, last night Anthony got a bit upset with me because I forgot one of his later in the day appointments, which meant he would be late for a dinner date we had with some friends.

When he first said — “I told you about that” — my knee-jerk reaction was “no you didn’t.”

Then I took a big breath, and thought to myself “Amy, take responsibility… after the gigantically full day you just had, you just forgot.”

I could feel the temperature rising, so I quickly reframed. Taking responsibility for it wasn’t difficult, I was happy to… and it meant I got to care-take the situation later to diffuse what had just transpired.

Without taking responsibility though, I would have followed what I felt (which was initial self-defense, honestly), rather than considering the option — which was to take in the fact through self-reflection, which led to self-responsibility… which lead to less upset.

Without peeling feelings off of facts, we may only have a hurt to speak from… a one-sided coin that hasn’t ingested the full picture yet.

Facts and feelings sure can work together to show us a broader perspective — that we may not yet have considered — when we get attached to one over the other.

Is there a situation in your life that’s asking to be seen for what it is, so feelings can also be taken into consideration… without turning things lop-sided?

If so, do things change when self-responsibility is the basis of how you approach it?
