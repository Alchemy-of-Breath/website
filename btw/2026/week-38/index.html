A note from Amy

From Hurt to Happiness

We all want happiness, and none of us want hurt, really.

But if authentic happiness is what we’re after (rather than manufactured happiness: “I’ll be happy when I have more money, lose weight, find the right partner” — etc), what if there’s another way?

And it’s not to say those things aren’t fundamentally important — and that they won’t bring us fulfillment on some levels.

Yet what happens when we have them, and the old ache is still there — and our happiness fades, flounders, or otherwise stops being fulfilling?

It may cause us to repeat the same old patterns in relationships that create pain, work situations bringing us down, and the echo of that old voice that still says “I’m not good enough.”

And then the chase is on. The never-ending pursuit of pleasure, a temporary fix to avoid feeling what’s brewing beneath the surface — the ever-elusive carrot we can’t seem to have.

Is there a pit of despair, pain, and deep hurt underneath it all, that robs your happiness like an invisible thief in the night?

Lost dreams. Forgotten priorities — that once made us tick — get sidetracked. Loss of love, and loved ones. There’s millions of reasons to be unhappy.

Why?

If the hurt — we may not even know we have — doesn’t get attention (so it can come out), it digs deeper like salt to a sore, until we unconsciously build a fortress around our heart, so we don’t have to feel the pain.

Trouble is, when we stop feeling, we’re protected not only from the hurt, yet from the happiness too.

Some of us blame, make it about someone or something else — our partner, the government, and proclaim “that’s just the way it is.”

And many of us take it out on ourselves… we self-punish to the point of no return, where it may feel like we have nothing left to live for, or to give, much less receive.

Imagine kicking a kid to the curb because they’re crying from such deep pain.

No. You wouldn’t do it.

Though that’s exactly what we do to ourselves when the hurt isn’t acknowledged. It’s like saying to a child:

Suck it up. Stop being such a baby.

You don’t deserve anything better anyway, look at everything you’ve done.

Outrun it — stay busy. Just ignore it, it’ll go away.

Essentially what we’re saying is that we aren’t worth anything else but suffering the same old pain program.

And on and on the mean talk goes, robbing us of joy, simple happiness, contentment, and peace inside.

Sound familiar? Most of us don’t even realize we’re doing it.

That’s what personally got me on my path. After so much destruction to my body and being — including losing my fertility at 15 — I knew death was close by in my late 20’s if I didn’t do something about it.

Thing is, I didn’t know I was even suffering until it got extreme, or any other way.

After some serious chaos too tender to talk about here, I became desperate enough to finally make it stop, and take responsibility for my victimhood.

Whether you’re unconsciously carrying a “death wish” like I was, or simply stuck and not knowing how to get traction… the hurt can be, ironically enough, a double-sided coin to happiness. We’re talking about real, lasting happiness — not the fake stuff based on circumstances.

This is why we breathe, consciously.

Developing a breath practice can be the difference between perpetuating the inner poverty of neglect we likely learned as a kid — and live in when we don’t know how to tend to what hurts — and the richness we can access when we finally stop running from it.

Whether you’ve been around our community for some time, or you’re new to Alchemy of Breath, and our unique style of breathwork, all you need to do is show up, breathe, and receive.

Be guided. Be nurtured. Let go of “the right and wrong way” and open up to what’s possible when we finally feel safe enough to — feel.

This Sunday, at our complimentary breathwork session that’s been going strong since 2015, you’ll experience the love, dedication, and craft of two of our facilitators who have so much to give, based on all they’ve felt and faced within themselves. I highly recommend attending both sessions if you can, and do forward this to your friends, or anyone you know who’s hurting. This could be a game changer.

My love and support to you!
