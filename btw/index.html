A note from Amy

Why You’re Worth It

Dear friend,

When I think about some of the things I settled for in my life in my younger years — especially in relationships — it takes me back a bit.

Big breath.

It seems life is one lesson after another in learning to know, and truly own, our own worth.

Why do we allow ourselves to be treated in ways that hurt us?

Why do we make excuses for behaviors — from people we love — that diminish us?

Why do we stay in situations that reinforce the story that we’re not enough?

These opportunities to look at how we value ourselves never seem to go away. If anything, they seem to become stronger, especially if we slip back into the grip of an all to common and universal story.

You know the one.

“I’m not good enough.”

After working with thousands of people over the years, this is one of the most common stories I hear again and again — something so many of us struggle with — and sometimes live under the spell of without even realizing it.

Does believing it make it true?

Perhaps not, though the results we’re getting may make us question what’s actually real. And that’s what matters, bottom line… do you like the results of what you’re getting? What do they tell you, in terms of your own value?

What if they are a mirror of what you actually believe, over what’s actually true?

I remember the last fight I had with an old boyfriend, just before Anthony and I came together.

It was one of those moments when my sense of self-worth had plummeted.

What I was experiencing in the ongoing conflict we shared, actually reflected something much deeper within me — that I blamed him for.

He was just a catalyst to show me how the unconscious “I’m not good enough” belief was keeping in the same loop of pain — and, not getting what I wanted.

It was like fighting myself and holding him responsible — like “drinking poison while waiting for the other to die.”

After our last fight, I heard a voice inside me that was loud and crystal clear. It said:

“YOU DESERVE SO MUCH MORE.”

It wasn’t just something I heard.

I felt it move through my entire body.

And like a light fully coming on, I saw everything differently.

It shook me out of the haze of the life I had been dragging myself through — somehow believing it was my job to carry someone who was emotionally unavailable — and who gained bigness by making me small.

Owning that was huge. Only I allowed that, no one else.

Letting go of that relationship, and the version of myself who was unconsciously invested in low self-value, ushered in an entirely new era of my life.

One that eventually brought me the man of my dreams.

But did that mean I was done learning about my worth?

Not at all.

What changed was that I knew I couldn’t go back.

I couldn’t go back to that way of seeing myself.

Instead, I became curious about the part of me that had made settling for less — normalized.

And that opened a much deeper question:

Why am I worth anything else?

Not because someone else says so, or I’ve achieved something, or because I’ve finally become “good enough.”

I finally got that my worth was never something I needed to earn in the first place.

This Sunday, let’s breathe into our worth together.

Every Sunday, people from around the world gather for our complimentary Breathe The World sessions in two time zone. It’s a practice that has reached more than 2 million people since 2015, and contributed to transforming that part of us that knows we deserve so much more.

It’s a very special week dear friends, as we have not just our facilitators leading you — yet our trainer team with Pablo and Tina Marie who will share with you their own journey around their worth — in the tenderness that’s so very true to who they are.

Wherever you are within yourself, come as you are.

Bring the old stories, the places where you’ve settled, and the part of you that may still wonder if you’re enough.

And let’s breathe together as we question old versions of ourselves and open to what becomes possible when we simply say:

Yes. I am worth it.

My deep love and support to you, friend.

Amy
Alchemy of Breath / ASHA, Tuscany
